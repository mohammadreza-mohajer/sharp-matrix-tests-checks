## Relation with Crystalline Realizations of 1-Motives

The earlier paper identifies the geometric exponent of a weight-splitting defect. Proposition 5.6 here gives its two-block linear-algebra interpretation and computes the full finite defect from the rational correction matrix. It distinguishes that particular defect from the universal Sylvester cokernel.

The new dimension problem measures how large an integral representation must be to detect a fixed group-ring divisibility defect. Neither a resultant bound nor the exponent of a fixed splitting quotient determines this dimension. The higher cyclotomic lattices connect the two questions by supplying both optimal detectors and explicit comparison quotients.

The earlier finite-field Frobenius realization is semisimple. The Jordan examples in this paper belong to the broader category of integral representations and permit a nonsemisimple diagonal block. The scope difference is stated in Section 5.1.

## Main originality assessment

The central focused result is the exact pair of dimension formulas in Theorem 8.6, with its uniform lower-bound proof and its attaining integral orders. For a fixed cyclotomic coefficient extension, the dimension saving is unbounded. The same orders have full Smith factors and closed Artin–Kummer period matrices; their elliptic tensor realization has a specified nonzero abelian factor.

The comparison above separates these claims from the nearest classical results. The primary-source searches did not locate this exact theorem package in the compared works. That finding supports the present positioning; it is not a proof that no older formulation exists.

In particular, the paper makes no new general-priority claim for the Sylvester equation, companion-matrix density, the rational comparison theorem for all 1-motives, ordinary CM decomposition, or the classical Iwasawa pairing.

## A precise point concerning Frisch

Only the first assertion of Theorem 6.3 is used for context. It applies over every domain. The separate irreducible-companion assertion has a stronger global hypothesis, corrected in the author's version 3; that assertion is not invoked over the local coefficient ring here.
